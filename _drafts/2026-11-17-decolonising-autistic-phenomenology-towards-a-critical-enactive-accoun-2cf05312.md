## Principais resultados

Resumo original da base bibliográfica (não traduzido): Recent years have seen a turn toward phenomenological, ecological, and enactive approaches to autism and neurodiversity, within the philosophy of psychiatry and cognitive science. These approaches challenge dominant neuro-reductionist and computational biomedical paradigms, centring embodiment, relationality, affectivity, and the constitutive role of sociomaterial environments in cognition. However, both phenomenological and ecological-enactive approaches are heterogeneous; not all escape the Eurocentric neuronormative assumptions underlying colonial modernity. This paper argues that, where classical phenomenology positions autism as a deviation from implicitly Western standards of 'neurotypical' embodiment and intersubjectivity, critical phenomenology and enactivism supply promising resources to reconceive autistic traits as intrinsically meaningful forms of sense-making - whilst interrogating the universality of categories such as 'autism' and 'neurotypicality.' Critical phenomenology draws attention to how power shapes embodiment, stretching the phenomenological method to confront its own complicity in Eurocentric neuronormativity. Enactivism goes further, providing an inclusive relational ontology of cognitive diversity that can facilitate dialogue with non-Western and Indigenous ways of knowing. Yet it risks becoming politically neutralised when inattentive to coloniality and intersectional oppression. Drawing insight from South Asian autistic experiences, I sketch out a critical-enactive framework oriented toward dismantling both neuronormative and Eurocentric (including logocentric and anthropocentric) assumptions in phenomenology and cognitive science. This decolonial approach paves the way for a pluralistic, intercultural understanding of autism - and embodied cognitive diversity, more broadly - doing justice to culturally and ecologically situated styles of sense-making, without essentialising neurological or cognitive differences.
